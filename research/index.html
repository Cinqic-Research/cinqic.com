02 — Current research

AAA — Accurate Autonomous Adaptation

AAA is Cinqic’s experimental research program on systems that learn from feedback, adapt when conditions change, keep what remains useful, and recognize when their knowledge is no longer adequate. Its tests are built so that the answer can be no.

Its current work is the Erudition Model for Juniper 1. Juniper 1 has three model components: a language model (OpenAI’s gpt-oss-20b, used unmodified), a world model that predicts the consequences of actions, and the Erudition Model, which decides whether, when, and how the other two should adapt. It then checks whether each change actually helped, keeps the ones that did, and rolls back the ones that did not. There is no separate decision model. The language model’s weights are never changed; it adapts only through a separate, versioned memory that can be removed.

The first experiments run in aaa.erudition.v0, a synthetic tool workspace whose behavior and vocabulary change without notice. Results, including failures and what remains unverified, are reported in the repository. Earlier AAA work, including the moving-dot benchmarks, the AAA-1K learners, and two Python-learning phases (aaa.python.v0 and aaa.python.v1), is kept as historical evidence. Its conclusions belong to those tasks and do not carry over to the new model.

Claim boundary

AAA makes no claim to general intelligence, understanding, or independent goal formation. Model size is not treated as progress; capacity is added only when the evidence justifies it.

03 — Retired research

Juniper LM 1

Juniper LM 1 was a controlled study of modernizing the original GPT-2 124M checkpoint while keeping its architecture, tokenizer, and size fixed. Development ended on October 4, 2026 (America/New_York).

The stock GPT-2 baseline, frozen benchmark versions, failed experiments, research logs, and provenance remain preserved as historical evidence. Retirement does not invalidate JuniperBench-Code v1.2 provenance.

Historical record

No Juniper LM 1 checkpoint was trained or released. The research record remains available as a concise historical summary.

04 — Retired research

Juniper Encoder

Juniper Encoder was a small encoder-only model investigated for routing requests among registered agents, tools, and skills. It was retired on September 15, 2026 with research complete: the configured benchmark matrix was executed in full, but held-out routing accuracy did not support deployment.

Historical record

No model was released and no production checkpoint was selected. The repository is public and read-only, retained for research history and reproducibility.

05 — Completed research

Juniper Math 1

Juniper Math 1 is a completed and retired research project. Its final conclusion found that the tested small architecture and training regimes learned meaningful component behaviors, but no Phase 8 checkpoint simultaneously met the project’s direct capability, tool-use, retention, and reliability gates.

Historical record

It is not an active model project, and no Phase 8 production or release checkpoint was approved.
